# **1\. The Billing Problem: The Non-Linear Cost of Cardiovascular Decay**

For medical billing professionals and utilization reviewers, coding for chronic hypertension and vascular disease using flat, linear risk assumptions consistently leads to revenue leakage. Clinical reality dictates that as an untreated patient’s arterial narrowing and blood viscosity worsen, the strain on institutional resources accelerates along a steep **cubic progression curve**.

If a hospital does not aggressively treat the underlying microvascular issues, patients rapidly shift into high-acuity DRG categories (e.g., acute heart failure, stroke, myocardial infarction). These categories carry massive operational costs that frequently exceed fixed insurance capitation caps, resulting in uncompensated hospital care.

To prevent this, our framework translates the patient's **Vascular Restriction Index (D)**—a composite index of blood pressure, blood thickness, and arterial diameter—into a predictable, billable **Total Annualized Management Cost (\\(C\_{\\text{total}}\\))** function.
